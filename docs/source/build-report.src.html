What changed, and what did not

Revision 1 assumed a Western seller. That assumption was wrong in a way that invalidates several recommendations outright — and, unexpectedly, makes the design brief easier rather than harder.

The seven that would sink it

1. Every call to action fails contrast. #A19E97 — "View Works", "Buy", "Sold", "Go to Checkout" — is 2.67:1 on white. No font size rescues it. (Unchanged from rev. 1.)

2. A one-page site cannot link to one painting. A #work-3 fragment never reaches the server. (Unchanged.)

3. Stripe is unavailable to a mainland-China seller — not merely PayPal-via-Stripe, the whole account. Verified against Stripe's own country list.

4. Airwallex will not give card acquiring to a mainland-China entity, and will not open an account for an individual at all. A Hong Kong entity turns it on.

5. Instagram is blocked in mainland China and is the draft's only contact channel — which was already a consumer-law problem in the EU and is now a reachability problem at home too.

6. The no-returns assumption is wrong in both markets. A unique pre-existing painting gets no exception under Chinese or EU law. The draft's "How to Buy" needs a returns policy it currently does not have.

7. China-origin paintings are not duty-free into the US. Section 301 List 4A adds 7.5%, and the $800 de minimis exemption is suspended, so every painting needs a customs entry. The site cannot tell US buyers there is no duty.

What survives untouched: the single-column editorial scroll, which is correct at six works and is the gallery idiom rather than the marketplace one; the decision to show prices; the tombstone line's quiet grey; and both typefaces, which are open-source and therefore self-hostable — the single most useful accident in the draft, as section 05 explains.

A-01BlockingWCAG 1.4.3 AA

Every call to action fails contrast — and it is the whole revenue path

Large-text relief does not apply: WCAG defines large as ≥24px regular or ≥18.5px bold, and these run at roughly 13px. The CTA text is under 60% of the required contrast. Underneath the compliance problem is a commercial one — quiet editorial retailers use approximately 30%-alpha black, close to this luminance, as their literal disabled state. Trained e-commerce eyes read this button as unavailable.

The fix keeps the aesthetic. Buy quietness with size, weight and whitespace rather than lightness.

.buy{ background:#2B2620; color:#fff; min-height:48px;
      font-size:.8125rem; letter-spacing:.09em; text-transform:uppercase; }
.buy:hover{ background:#8B4A3C; }        /* 6.68:1 */
.quiet-link{ color:#2B2620; text-decoration:underline;
      text-decoration-color:#989491;     /* 3.01:1 hairline */
      text-underline-offset:.28em; }

No accent on this palette can be a colour-only link

Technique G183 requires 3:1 between an inline link and surrounding prose when colour alone distinguishes it. Measured against body text #5C5346: oxblood 1.13:1, sanguine 1.19:1, ultramarine 1.32:1, cobalt 1.09:1. Not one is close, and this holds for every accent proposed in section 02 as well. Underlines on body-copy links are structural, not stylistic.

Standalone links — the four nav items, the Buy control — are exempt, because they do not sit inside surrounding static text. The nav bar stays clean.

#767676 is the canonical "lightest grey that passes AA on white." On this draft's true #FFFFFF it passes — at 4.54:1, with 0.04 to spare. 02

Two masters, quietly

The brief: a die-hard admiration for Leonardo and an innate motivation from van Gogh, carried in colour, ground, navigation and motion — never announced. The test applied throughout: a visitor who knows nothing should feel only that the site is calm and well made; a visitor who knows the work should feel recognised.

The thesis, and why it is not decoration

Leonardo's instruction to paint "without lines or borders, in the manner of smoke" — sfumato — and the Chinese 没骨 (mògǔ, "boneless") tradition of painting without contour lines are the same idea reached independently, five thousand miles and some centuries apart. For a Chinese painter whose site must speak to both audiences, that is not a decorative motif. It is the organising principle, and it is legible from either side without translation.

So the homage is structural: a site with no hard edges. Section boundaries dissolve rather than rule. That single decision carries Leonardo, carries 没骨, and reads to everyone else simply as restraint.

D-01Adopt

The Leonardo reference is already in the draft — it just needs naming

Sanguine, the red chalk Leonardo drew in for most of his life, computes to 6.33:1 on white. The oxblood the artist independently chose computes to 6.68:1. They are, for practical purposes, the same colour.

The recommendation is therefore to change nothing and document it. Keep #8B4A3C exactly as drafted; record in the design tokens that it is the sanguine note. This is the single subtlest move available, because the homage was arrived at by eye rather than applied afterwards — which is what makes it credible.

Add ultramarine #2E3A8C at 10:1 as the secondary accent, held in reserve for exactly one job (the Tribute section, which is itself about admiration for other artists). Two accents, one warm and one cold, is the complementary pairing van Gogh wrote about constantly — and it is the only place blue appears on the site.

Verified: Starry Night pigment analysis — "the sky was painted with ultramarine and cobalt blue, and for the stars and the moon, van Gogh employed the rare pigment indian yellow together with zinc yellow." Contrast values computed this session.

D-02Adopt

Dissolve the section rules — sfumato as a structural decision, not a texture

The draft separates its five sections with 1px hairlines at #E1E0DF. Replacing each with a tall, very shallow gradient band does three things at once: it enacts "without lines or borders", it reads as 没骨 to a Chinese visitor, and it removes a 1.32:1 line that was doing no accessibility work anyway.

.section-edge{
  height:9rem;
  background:linear-gradient(in oklab, #FFFFFF 0%, #FAF7F2 55%, #FFFFFF 100%);
}

Two implementation notes that matter. Interpolate in oklab — sRGB interpolation produces a visible grey dip through the midpoint on low-saturation gradients, which is precisely the case here. And a gradient this shallow across this large an area will band on 8-bit displays; the fix is a fixed-position SVG feTurbulence noise overlay at very low opacity, which dithers the steps away.

Tint the bands from the pigment set, one per section, at the washed-out end: warm sfumato #FAF7F2 for Original Works, #F4F1F6 violet for Tribute, blue-paper #EEF1F5 for About. Body text still measures 6.67–7.07:1 on all three, so the quiet is free.

The measured facts justify the proportion, not just the idea. X-ray fluorescence modelling of the Mona Lisa puts the total glaze at its darkest at 40 ± 10 µm, thinning to under 5 µm where the shadow begins — and that taper is spread over 5.5 cm of picture surface. The lead white underneath stays flat at 40–45 µm, so the entire transition is carried by the glaze wedge alone.

Two design consequences follow directly. The ratio of transition length to layer depth is roughly 1,400:1 — which is why a sfumato band has to be tall and shallow rather than a tight fade; a 9rem band across a 1200px column is close to the same proportion. And the transition is carried by one continuous element, not by stacking many: resist the urge to layer three gradients.

Verified: de Viguerie et al., Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 49(35), 6125–6128 (2010), and the underlying 2009 thesis. Note: XRF returns total attenuating thickness, so the authors state they cannot even validate a two-glaze model — any "thirty layers" figure is untraceable and should not appear in the documentation.

D-03Adopt

Hatch the other way

Leonardo was left-handed — his mirror writing is uncontested — and his hatching therefore runs upper-left to lower-right, the mirror of a right-hander's stroke. It is one of the standard tells used to attribute sheets to his hand. A hatching texture angled his way costs nothing and rewards only the person who would notice.

Draw it as SVG rather than a CSS gradient, because the angle sign is unambiguous in path coordinates and easy to get backwards in repeating-linear-gradient:

<pattern id="hatch" width="7" height="7" patternUnits="userSpaceOnUse"
         patternTransform="rotate(-45)">
  <line x1="0" y1="0" x2="0" y2="7" stroke="#8B4A3C" stroke-opacity=".22"/>
</pattern>

Use it in one place only, very faintly — behind the Tribute eyebrow label, or as the sold-state fill. Never as a page background.

Left-handedness: verified. Direction: measured, not cited. No text source could be retrieved (the Royal Collection, Britannica and NGA all refuse automated fetch), so the angle was established by structure-tensor analysis of the drawings themselves, validated first against synthetic hatch fields of known angle. Measured spread 40–52°; two chalk sheets ran the other way, and hatching that follows curved form has no single global angle. Present it as a measurement, not a citation.

D-04High

The van Gogh note is a colour that is no longer there

This is the strongest available van Gogh reference, and it is not a swirl.

Van Gogh wrote to Theo of The Bedroom: "The walls are of a pale violet." The painting today reads blue, because the red lake pigment he mixed in has faded in the intervening century. The work we look at is not the work he chose. That is a real, documented, quietly devastating fact about painting and time — and it is exactly what a site selling original physical objects is implicitly arguing about.

Implementation: use the pale violet #9B8AA6 — his stated colour, not the faded one — as the hairline and rule colour throughout, and as the ::selection background. It measures 3.19:1, which clears the 3:1 non-text threshold for a rule that carries meaning. Nobody will decode it. It will simply be the one slightly unusual grey on the site.

And he knew. From Arles, on his own palette — the chromes, Prussian blue, emerald, the crimson lakes:

All the colours that the Impressionists have brought into fashion are unstable, so there is all the more reason not to be afraid to lay them on too crudely — time will tone them down only too much.

That is the whole argument of this site in one sentence by someone else: a painting is a physical object that keeps changing after it leaves the studio, and that is a reason to be bolder rather than more careful. It belongs on the About page far more than any biographical note about ears or asylums.

It also earns the single true sentence about why the artist photographs and colour-manages carefully — connecting the homage to finding I-01 rather than leaving it as ornament.

Verified: van Gogh to Theo, letter 476 (old numbering), Arles; and his own description of the Bedroom walls as "pale violet". The letters are fully published and need no myth.

D-05Adopt

Motion: an easing curve with a source, not a swirl

Aragón, Naumis, Bai, Torres and Maini, Turbulent luminance in impassioned van Gogh paintings, found that the probability distribution of luminance fluctuations in his late work matches the PDF of velocity differences in Kolmogorov turbulence. The honest translation of that is not a rotating background. It is a statement about how energy distributes — many small fluctuations, rare large ones.

As an easing curve that means: leave quickly, settle slowly, and never overshoot.

--ease-settle: cubic-bezier(.16,.84,.30,1);   /* hovers, fades */
--ease-enter:  cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1);     /* view transitions */

Apply only to opacity and the sfumato bands. Honesty note: no easing function is derivable from a turbulence PDF in any rigorous sense. These are ordinary well-behaved curves chosen to match the character the paper describes. Anyone claiming a mathematical derivation is overselling it.

Everything gated behind @media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference). Six viewport-heights of large paintings is already a long scroll; decorative easing on top of it is a vestibular risk, not a flourish.

Verified: arXiv physics/0606246 — title, authors and abstract read this session. The paper's claims have been contested in later fluid-dynamics literature; do not present it as settled.

D-06Adopt

Use the proportions Leonardo actually wrote down — they are whole numbers, not φ

The golden-ratio story is the wrong homage. The one proportional system documented in Leonardo's own hand, on the Vitruvian Man sheet, is entirely rational: 4 fingers = 1 palm; 4 palms = 1 foot; 6 palms = 1 cubit; 4 cubits = the height of a man. Then, as fractions of total height — hairline to chin 1/10, chin to crown 1/8, breast to crown 1/6, nipples to crown 1/4, the hand 1/10, the foot 1/7. There is no φ anywhere on the sheet.

That is a usable modular scale, and it is better than φ for typography because the steps are coarse enough to be legible as hierarchy. Taking the draft's own 16.8px body as the unit and treating the hero as the "height":

The draft is already within a few percent of it at every step. The recommendation is to snap the six sizes onto the rational scale and record why — which costs an afternoon and makes the type system explicable rather than arbitrary.

The φ myth

Cut, and why — the myth filter

  • Golden-ratio grid. Cut, and replaced by D-06. The claim that Leonardo composed the Mona Lisa or Last Supper on φ is unsupported; illustrating Pacioli's De divina proportione establishes that he drew the solids, not that he composed on the ratio.
  • "Leonardo invented sfumato", and the word itself. Cut from any copy. Cennino Cennini described blending shadows "like smoke" a century earlier, and Leonardo never used the noun sfumato — a full-text search of the Trattato returns none. What he wrote is "ad uso di fumo", in the passage instructing that lights and shadows be united "senza tratti o segni" — without strokes or lines. Quote that if anything is quoted.
  • Impasto texture on the UI. Cut. A raised, light-catching surface is the one thing a flat screen cannot honestly imitate, and faking it with drop shadows is precisely the kitsch the brief forbids. The real impasto belongs in the photography — the raking-light detail crop in finding N-06 — where it is a photograph of a real surface rather than a CSS impression of one.
  • Any yellow as text or UI. Cut on measurement. Indian yellow is 2.10:1 and zinc yellow 1.53:1 on white. They are genuine Starry Night pigments and are unusable for anything but a mark inside an image.
  • Sampling colours off the paintings themselves. Cut — and this is the trap most likely to recreate the original failure. Sampled from a reproduction, the Bedroom wall as it survives today is #8E9EA1 at 2.78:1, and the Sunflowers chrome yellow is #B19E14 at 2.70:1. Both sit within a hair of the #A19E97 2.67:1 failure this whole report opens with. Desaturated mid-tones lifted off paintings are exactly where low contrast comes back in. Use nominal pigment values, darkened to pass, and label which you mean — the blues in section 02 are nominal, not as-painted; the as-painted Starry Night sky samples at #4E6991 (5.59:1).
  • "He sold only one painting" / absinthe / digitalis. Cut as framing for any About copy. The first is the most repeated van Gogh claim and is not accurate; the palette theories are unsupported speculation. If the site says anything about him, quote the letters — they are fully published and need no myth.
03

Reaching both markets

The artist is in mainland China and wants the domestic and international audiences weighted equally. Most of this section is measurement rather than opinion — reachability from inside China is testable, and was tested.

B-01Blocking

Stripe is not available, and Airwallex needs a Hong Kong entity

Stripe: mainland China is absent from Stripe's supported business countries. Hong Kong, Singapore and Japan are present. This is not the rev. 1 finding about PayPal — it is the whole account.

Airwallex looked like the answer and is more restricted than it appears. A mainland entity can open an account for Global Accounts, FX and transfers — but card acquiring is excluded in Airwallex's own Chinese-language terms: 「以下服务仅针对非中国主体客户适用」. Card schemes list business locations of AU, CA, CH, EEA, HK, IL, JP, MX, MY, SG, NZ, UK, US — no CN. And Airwallex does not open accounts for individuals at all: "we're unable to offer personal accounts to individuals at this time."

A Hong Kong sole proprietorship flips everything on — a Business Registration certificate is enough, no incorporation required. That unlocks the free Explore plan with no-code payment links, international cards at 3.60% + HK$2.35, and Alipay and WeChat Pay as merchant payment methods — which is how the domestic half of the audience gets served from the same checkout.

It also unlocks repatriation: Airwallex supports local CNY transfer for "customers without a registered business in mainland China… e-commerce or retail sellers with a Chinese national legal representative or UBO, and exporting goods from mainland China." That describes this business exactly.

Rejected: Payoneer Checkout is Stripe-powered, excludes mainland China explicitly, and requires "monthly webstore volumes above USD $20,000" — roughly 130× this business. PingPong and LianLian both have real independent-site acquiring, but neither publishes pricing or eligibility and both are sales-led; do not design around them without a written quote.

Open item: whether Airwallex approves a HK sole proprietorship for the Payments product specifically. The entity list verified is for account opening; Payments approval is a separate discretionary review that includes checking the live site. Ask before incorporating.

B-02High

There is a launch-now path that needs no company at all

A Hong Kong entity is the grown-up answer. It should not block launch, because Chinese law is unusually accommodating at this scale.

No business registration is required. SAMR Order 37 Art. 8 ¶3: an individual doing online trade with annual turnover not exceeding CNY 100,000 is exempt from 市场主体登记. Six paintings at $60–$440 is roughly CNY 19,000 — an order of magnitude below.

No foreign-exchange quota is consumed. 汇发〔2020〕14号 Art. 66(2) lets a domestic individual settle cross-border e-commerce receipts through her own FX account without touching the USD 50,000 annual facilitation quota, on proof of transaction amount or transaction electronic information. Settlement through a licensed payment institution is separately exempt under 汇发〔2019〕13号 Art. 38.

She files nothing herself. 汇发〔2022〕22号 Art. 8 exempts resident individuals from balance-of-payments declaration detail for single transactions at or below USD 10,000 equivalent. Every sale here is far below.

She is VAT-exempt — monthly sales under CNY 100,000, in force to 31 December 2027.

So: launch with PayPal plus a personal Alipay/WeChat route, keep clean order and shipping records, and stand up the Hong Kong entity when volume justifies it. What she must not do is route sales proceeds as gifts or family remittance, split transactions, or use informal currency swaps — the last is 私自买卖外汇 under Art. 45 of the FX Regulations, and published enforcement puts the penalty on the offender's PBOC credit file.

B-05Blocking

She cannot refuse returns — in either market

This is the unwelcome headline, and the draft's "How to Buy" currently says nothing about it.

China: CPL Art. 25 gives a 7-day no-reason return on distance sales. The exceptions are a closed list — SAIC Order 90 Art. 6 excludes only goods made to the consumer's order (定作), perishables, unsealed digital media and delivered periodicals; Art. 7 adds three narrow categories, none of which fit. The 2024 Implementing Regulations Art. 19 then expressly forbid a trader unilaterally expanding the excluded list, and ban pre-ticked or default exclusion consent.

EU: CRD Art. 16(c) excepts goods "made to the consumer's specifications or clearly personalised." Uniqueness is not personalisation — the test is whether the buyer specified it. A pre-existing painting chosen from a catalogue of six fails that test. Art. 16(k) covers public auctions, not fixed-price sales. EU buyers get the full 14 days.

A "governed by PRC law" clause does not help. Rome I Art. 6(2) prevents a choice of law from depriving a consumer of protections that cannot be derogated from under their home law, where the trader directs activity to that country. Offering EU shipping at checkout is directing activity.

Recommendation: one worldwide 14-day no-reason return. It over-satisfies the Chinese 7 days, removes any need to geo-detect buyers, and avoids a 7-day policy reading as an attempt to shorten the EU right. Publish alongside it: a tight condition standard (original packaging, certificate of authenticity, unframed, no re-varnishing), that the buyer pays return postage — legitimate in both regimes, but only if stated in advance — and refund within 7 days of receiving the work back.

Commissions are the one genuine exception under both regimes, and would need flagging at the point of sale with a per-order confirmation.

Correction to an earlier assumption: the Chinese measure is 工商总局令第90号, not No. 88. Sources read from gov.cn and the EU Publications Office this session. Not legal advice.

B-06Blocking

Instagram cannot be the only contact channel — for three separate reasons

It is blocked in mainland China, so half the intended audience cannot use it. The EU Consumer Rights Directive Art. 6(1)(c) requires a geographical address, telephone number and email address for distance contracts — other online channels are an addition, never a substitute. And Chinese law requires the same disclosure from a different direction.

SAMR Order 37 Art. 12 gives the exact sentence an unregistered individual seller must publish, alongside her real operating address and contact details:

个人从事零星小额交易活动，依法不需要办理市场主体登记

One address / phone / email block satisfies all three regimes. Add the Chinese self-declaration sentence beside it, and pair Instagram with WeChat and Xiaohongshu rather than replacing it.

Then the sentence that does the most work on the page:

If any part of this site stops you buying a painting, email [address]. I'll describe the work to you, take your order, and arrange payment and shipping personally — usually within two working days.

That satisfies the CRD, provides the alternative access route US law accepts, discharges the UK anticipatory duty — and lets a blind collector actually buy the painting.

B-07High

PIPL applies, but the compliance burden is genuinely small

She processes personal data from inside China, so PIPL Art. 3 ¶1 applies directly. Two things follow that are cheaper than expected:

  • No consent checkbox for the order. Art. 13(1)(2) permits processing necessary to conclude or perform a contract, and Art. 13's closing paragraph switches off the consent requirement for it. A just-in-time notice at the email field is the correct pattern. Consent is needed only for the newsletter — unticked, separate, non-blocking.
  • No PRC representative and no regulator filing. Art. 53 applies by its own terms only to handlers outside China. Hosting on Cloudflare does not convert her into an overseas handler.
  • No CAC security assessment, standard contract or certification for the overseas hosting. The 2024 Data Cross-Border Flow Provisions Art. 5(1) exempts transfers necessary to perform a contract — naming 跨境购物 and 跨境寄递 explicitly — and Art. 5(4) exempts anyone under 100,000 individuals per year.

What remains: a privacy page in Chinese and English naming her as handler, the categories collected, purposes, a concrete retention period, how to exercise rights, and the overseas recipients. Plus a one-page impact assessment for the transfer, retained three years.

B-08BlockingSite copy

China-origin paintings are not duty-free into the US — the draft must not say they are

Original paintings under HS 9701 carry a Free base rate, which is where the "art is duty-free" belief comes from. For a China-origin shipment in 2026 that is wrong, and the error would land on the buyer as a surprise bill.

  • Section 301 List 4A applies. HTSUS heading 9903.88.15 imposes +7.5% on products of China, and US note 20(s)(i) enumerates 9701.91.00 — contemporary paintings — among the covered subheadings. The product exclusion that once covered paintings is marked expired. There is no informational-materials carve-out in Section 301.
  • De minimis is suspended. The $800 exemption no longer applies to mail or other modes, so every $300 painting now needs a customs entry filed. Only the ≤$100 bona fide gift and ≤$200 traveller exemptions survive.
  • The IEEPA tariffs are gone — struck down and revoked in February 2026 — and artworks were separately carved out of them as "informational materials" under 50 U.S.C. §1702(b)(3). That relief does not extend to Section 301.
  • The 2026 forced-labour Section 301 action does not add to this — heading 9903.05.86 exempts 9701.21.00–9701.99.00.

Practical effect on a $300 painting to a US buyer: $0 base duty, +$22.50 Section 301, a few dollars of CBP fees, plus the carrier's brokerage or disbursement charge. Roughly 8–12% on top of the price.

Site copy must say "US buyers pay approximately 7.5% import duty plus customs handling," not "duty-free" and not a bare "buyer pays any duties." Surprise customs bills are a leading cause of cross-border art complaints, and this one is predictable enough to quote.

The UK is the opposite case and is safe to state. Queried against the UK Trade Tariff API for 9701910000 filtered to China: third-country duty 0.00%, geographical area ERGA OMNES — the measure attaches to the commodity code, not the country — with VAT measures of 5% and 20% present, confirming the reduced rate for works of art. Origin does not change UK treatment.

Verified: HTSUS Chapter 99 Revision 19 (2026); Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump (20 Feb 2026) and EO 14389; CBP de minimis final rules of 24 June 2026; UK Trade Tariff API. Not verified: the EU reduced art VAT rates (France 5.5%, Germany 7%, Italy 5%) — see section 07.

Bilingual mechanics — and the donation pledge

Chinese webfonts are a different order of problem. A full CJK face is 5–10 MB where the Latin pair is 80 KB. The workable pattern for a six-page site is unicode-range splitting with per-page subsetting, or — more honest at this scale — a system-font stack for Chinese text and the self-hosted Latin pair for everything else. This is an open item for Phase 2, not a solved one.

The 10% pledge needs rewording regardless of jurisdiction. "10% of each order goes toward research" is a per-transaction consumer promise; "I personally donate 10% of my profits each quarter to [named organisation]" is a personal pledge. The US commercial-co-venture exposure that drove this in rev. 1 largely falls away for a China-based seller, but the reputational logic does not: name the recipient, publish a receipts page with a running total, and state the cadence. Cross-border individual donations out of China add friction worth checking before naming an overseas recipient.

N-01Blocking

Ship seven documents, not one

A #work-3 fragment is never sent to the server — RFC 3986 §3.5 — so it can carry no per-work title, description, canonical, og:image, structured data or server-side analytics. Google's guidance is explicit: "Don't use fragments to change the content of a page."

Keep / as the editorial scroll exactly as drafted, add /works/{slug} × 6 generated from one artworks.json, each with a VisualArtwork + Offer JSON-LD block of about fifteen lines.

N-06High

Scale is the biggest unanswered question, and the cheap answer beats the expensive one

Baymard finds 42% of users try to judge size from the page images, and 37% of sites give them nothing to judge against. Nobody can infer 40×50cm from a crop.

Skip AR: <model-viewer> is 290 KB gzipped, WebXR has no iOS Safari support, and Artsy's own engineering write-up documents the fatal flaw — ARKit plane detection fails on blank walls, exactly where a painting goes. Saatchi's WebAR route is gone; 8th Wall's hosted platform retired in February 2026.

Three tiers, cheapest first: one in-situ wall photograph per painting (a camera and an afternoon); a build-time SVG scale diagram under the dimension line — Artsy's own "View in Room" is about 120 lines of CSS compositing with no AR and no library, and their visible "8 ft" bar deliberately reframes it as a diagram, which is the on-brand move here; and optionally a "See it on a wall" dialog with one tape-measured room photo.

Alongside this, one raking-light detail crop per painting — the honest home for the impasto idea cut in section 02. Baymard calls these "textural images" and notes they can be "a decisive purchasing factor." Shoot the texture at a glancing angle so impasto casts real micro-shadow; a flat-lit close-up conveys nothing a crop wouldn't.

Accessibility and imaging

Condensed from rev. 1. The legal framing has shifted — the European Accessibility Act's microenterprise exemption still applies, and a China-based sole trader is further from US litigation risk than a US one — but the work is worth doing on commercial grounds regardless.

A-04BlockingWCAG 1.1.1 A

Alt text for artwork: three layers, and the 125-character limit is folklore

There is no character limit in the HTML spec or WCAG; the myth traces to a misreading of how JAWS splits long alt text. Use Cooper Hewitt's citable number instead: approximately fifteen words, a sentence fragment, no full stop.

LayerMarkupContentWho sees it
Short altalt="…"~15 words: medium + dominant visual event + paletteScreen readers
Caption<figcaption>Title, year, medium, dimensions, priceEveryone
Extended<details> in the figure80–250 words: composition, palette, brushwork, scaleEveryone

Make the long description visible. W3C's own advice is to "make long descriptions available to everyone" — wider audience, indexable, and it sells the work. Do not use aria-describedby for the 200-word payload; screen readers flatten it into one uninterruptible string. longdesc is obsolete.

The guidance genuinely disagrees — DIAGRAM Center says never interpret, Cooper Hewitt says interpret when it aids visual understanding. For an artist's own gallery Cooper Hewitt is right, but keep interpretation textually separable: label it "Artist's note:", put it last, and never let intent replace observation.

Spend real words on technique and surface and give scale by embodied analogy. On AI drafting: fine as a first pass, never as shipped text — a model cannot know the true medium, dimensions, title or intent, and a wrong description is worse than none because it burns trust in the channel.

A-05High

"Buy" passes SC 2.4.4 only if the title and price sit in the same list item as the link. Wrap each work in an <li> and it is satisfied. But a screen-reader user opening NVDA's Elements List still gets "Buy, Buy, Buy, Buy, Buy, Buy":

A visually-hidden span rather than aria-label: it guarantees SC 2.5.3 structurally, makes voice control work, and survives browser translation — which matters more than usual on a bilingual site.

For sold works, remove the link entirely. There is no state to communicate. disabled drops it from the tab order so a keyboard user silently skips it; aria-disabled keeps it focusable but does not prevent activation, so a link with a live href announces "dimmed" and then navigates anyway.

I-01Blocking

Convert to sRGB and embed the profile — the build will strip it by default

The asymmetry decides it. Every modern browser assumes an untagged image is sRGB. So a tagged sRGB file that loses its profile renders correctly; a Display P3 file that loses its profile renders as heavily oversaturated sRGB. sRGB fails safe; P3 fails loud. Oil, watercolour and graphite pigments fall overwhelmingly inside sRGB anyway.

The trap: sharp — under Astro, Next and most image CDNs — states "By default all metadata will be removed." Force it:

sharp(input).toColourspace('srgb').withIccProfile('srgb')

This is where finding D-04 earns its keep. A site whose rule colour is van Gogh's stated violet, on a page that explains why the artist colour-manages carefully, is making the homage do actual work.

I-02High

Never let AVIF subsample chroma on a painting

The AVIF spec leaves chroma upsampling decoder-dependent, so 4:2:0 or 4:2:2 can render with visible colour-edge distortion depending on the viewer's decoder — nearest-neighbour upsampling is spec-valid. That maps precisely onto glazes, craquelure and low-contrast gradients.

.avif({ quality: 68, chromaSubsampling: '4:4:4', effort: 6 })

The strategic point matters more: with six images, file size is not the constraint. AVIF's advantage over JPEG shrinks below 25% above 1.22 bpp — exactly the quality range a gallery should work in. Use generous quality, inspect every encode at 100%. Ship AVIF + WebP; drop JPEG.

Cross-border bandwidth is the real performance lever for Chinese visitors, not the font and not the CDN. Getting six heroes from ~4 MB to ~250 KB each outweighs everything else combined. Two attributes carry most of it: width and height on every <img>, which is the difference between CLS 0 and CLS 0.4 — and note that lazy images without them default to 0×0, which can make the browser load all six at once.

I-03Adopt

Resolution limiting is the only image protection that works

Right-click blocking, user-select:none and canvas rendering are all defeated by a screenshot. They cost real accessibility, break "open image in new tab" — which serious buyers use to inspect work — and signal mistrust to someone being asked for $440.
